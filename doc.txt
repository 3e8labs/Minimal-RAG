RAG in Pure C — sample document

This is a small test document used to validate the chunking and embedding pipeline.
It intentionally mixes a few topics so retrieval has something to separate.

Section 1: Cats and dogs
I love cats. Cats are curious, independent, and great companions.
I also love dogs. Dogs are playful, loyal, and often enjoy long walks.

Section 2: The stock market
The stock market can be volatile. Prices move due to earnings, interest rates,
and expectations about the future. Diversification helps manage risk.

Section 3: Cooking
When making pasta, salt the water generously and cook until al dente.
Finish the pasta in the sauce so it absorbs flavor.

Section 4: Computers
In C, memory ownership matters. If a function allocates with malloc, the caller
must free. Clear conventions make programs easier to reason about.

Section 5: Space and physics
Light from distant stars takes years to reach Earth, so astronomy often looks back in time.
Gravity shapes the motion of planets and the structure of galaxies, and small changes in
initial conditions can lead to very different outcomes.

Section 6: Gardening
Healthy soil has good structure and holds moisture without becoming waterlogged. Compost adds
organic matter and helps plants grow steadily. Some plants prefer full sun, while others thrive
in partial shade, and paying attention to seasons can improve results.

Section 7: Notes on retrieval
When you build a RAG system, you want chunks to be small enough to retrieve precisely, but large
enough to preserve context. Overlap helps avoid cutting a sentence in half, but too much overlap
creates near-duplicates that can crowd out diverse results. In practice you tune chunk size,
overlap, and top-k together.

Section 8: More cooking details (keep together)
For a simple tomato sauce, gently cook garlic in olive oil, add crushed tomatoes, and simmer until
it thickens. For creamy sauces, emulsify fat and water: for example, stir pasta water into cheese
and butter off the heat. Taste and adjust with salt and acid. For baking, weighing ingredients is
more consistent than using measuring cups. Resting dough improves texture because flour hydrates
and gluten relaxes.

Section 9: Networking basics
HTTP is a request-response protocol. A client sends a request with a method (GET/POST), headers,
and optional body; a server returns a status code, headers, and a body. Latency dominates for small
payloads, so batching work can help. In local RAG, we call an LLM server over HTTP to avoid linking
against a C++ runtime.

Section 10: Music and practice
Practicing an instrument benefits from slow repetition and deliberate focus. Short sessions with
clear goals often beat long unfocused ones. Recording yourself can reveal timing and tone issues
that are hard to notice while playing.

Section 11: More computers
Programs fail in predictable ways: null pointers, off-by-one errors, and ownership mismatches.
Consistent interfaces reduce bugs. For example, returning a heap allocation and requiring the
caller to free it is a common C convention that keeps responsibilities clear.
